## 1. Youth training after the 2025 aid cuts

### LinkedIn (EN)
In 2025, a $25 million youth and community programme working in 22 of Tunisia's 24 governorates was cancelled mid-way.

Most US aid stopped almost overnight. Tunisian associations running youth programmes felt it first.

The money didn't vanish. It moved. UNDP, the EU and German-funded programmes still fund youth skills in Tunisia, but they now ask a different question: not "how many did you train?" but "how many got a job?"

What still works on a smaller budget:
→ Cut hours, never the practice
→ Train peer trainers, not only participants
→ Bring companies in early (the CNFCPP reimburses their training costs)
→ Measure one result for three months, properly

Smaller budgets are not a reason to train worse. They're a reason to stop doing the parts that never worked.

## 2. Cash on delivery: cut refused orders

### LinkedIn (EN)
The biggest cost for most Tunisian online stores isn't ads. It's the parcels that come back.

Cash on delivery is still around 70% of online orders in Tunisia, and sellers report 15 to 30% of them refused at the door.

Every refused parcel = delivery paid (often twice) + stock stuck for days + a delivery partner who trusts you a bit less.

Most refusals are designed in by the store:
→ A total that only appears at the door
→ Photos that don't match the product
→ No confirmation call or message
→ "Fast delivery" that takes five days

Seven fixes, most of them free, in the article. Link in the first comment.

The number to watch isn't orders. It's orders delivered and kept.
