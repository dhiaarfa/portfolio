Color Psychology Guide

How to choose brand colours that set the right expectation, and how to apply them so your brand looks like one brand everywhere. With 12 ready-to-use palettes and their HEX codes.

By Mohamed Dhia Arfa · Zia Studio · dhia-portfolio.com

Colour sets the expectation. Consistency keeps the promise.

Colour psychology is not magic: a blue logo won't make a business trustworthy, and red won't force anyone to buy. What colour does is set an expectation before anyone reads a word. Trust is built when the experience matches that expectation, and when the same colours appear the same way on every post, package and sign.

When a brand feels "off", the problem is rarely the colour choice. It is usually that there are eleven slightly different versions of it.

Cultural context matters. Brands working across Arabic, French and English audiences should test colours with real customers. In Tunisia, red and white evoke the flag, green carries religious weight, and gold and blue are strongly tied to tradition and the coast. These can be strengths, if they are chosen on purpose.

Give every colour a role

A brand needs roles, not a rainbow. When everything is a "brand colour", nothing guides the eye, and the most important button gets lost.

60% neutrals: backgrounds, text
30% primary
10%

A common starting split: about 60% neutrals, 30% primary, 10% accent. The accent is reserved for what you want clicked or noticed.

RoleUsed forYour HEX
PrimaryLogo, key brand moments
NeutralsMost surfaces and all body text
AccentCalls to action, highlights only
Status (optional)Success, warning, error in apps and forms

Check contrast before you ship

  • Body text: contrast ratio of at least 4.5 : 1 against its background (WCAG AA)
  • Large text and icons: at least 3 : 1
  • Never put text on yellow, light green or light orange without checking
  • Check your palette in greyscale: if two colours become the same grey, don't rely on colour alone

Lock it in

  • One exact HEX per colour (no "roughly this green")
  • RGB for screens, CMYK and Pantone references for print
  • The same values in Canva, Figma, your website and your printer's file
  • A one-page sheet anyone on the team can find
