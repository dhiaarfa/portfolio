Brand Consistency Scorecard

Does your brand look like one brand everywhere, from Instagram to packaging? Score 20 points in 10 minutes and see exactly where it breaks.

By Mohamed Dhia Arfa · Zia Studio · dhia-portfolio.com

For each line, tick one box: 0 = no, 1 = partly, 2 = yes, every time. Open your last 9 posts, your packaging, your shop sign and your website side by side before you start.

Check012
Logo
The same logo file is used everywhere (no old versions, no screenshots)
There is a version that works on dark and on light backgrounds
The logo is never stretched, recoloured or placed on busy photos
Colour
Your colours have exact HEX codes that everyone uses
Print and screen colours look like the same colours
One accent colour is reserved for what you want people to click or notice
Typography
Two typefaces at most, used the same way each time
Arabic and Latin text are paired on purpose (matching weight and size)
Social media
Your last 9 posts look like they come from the same brand
You post from templates, not from scratch each time
Check012
Packaging, print & signage
Packaging, menu or flyers use the same colours and type as your social media
Your shop sign or stand matches your logo files
Website & profiles
Your website, Facebook and Instagram profile pictures and covers match
Your Google Business profile shows current logo, photos and hours
Voice
Your tone is the same in Arabic, French and English posts
You describe what you do in the same one sentence everywhere
System
A one-page brand sheet exists and the whole team can find it
New staff or freelancers get the brand files before they start
Someone is responsible for checking new materials before they go out
You have reviewed your brand in the last 12 months

Your score (out of 40)

0-19 · ScatteredPeople see a different brand on each channel. Start with logo files and HEX codes: they fix the most visible problems first.
20-31 · RecognisableThe basics hold, but templates and a brand sheet are missing. Consistency depends on who made the post.
32-40 · ConsistentA real system. Keep it reviewed yearly and brief every new collaborator.
Fix the lowest area first. Consistency is judged by the weakest touchpoint: one off-brand flyer undoes ten good posts.
